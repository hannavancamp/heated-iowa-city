The Best Hot Yoga in Iowa City

The best studio for you depends on how much heat you want, how far you are willing to travel, and what kind of class keeps you coming back. Here are three studios in the Iowa City area, compared on the things beginners ask about most.

Three studios at a glance

Ember Hot Yoga

Downtown Iowa City, a short walk from campus. Busy, energetic, and the easiest option if you do not have a car.

  • Room temperature 95 to 105°F
  • Class styles Hot vinyasa, Heated Basics, yoga sculpt
  • Beginner class Heated Basics, Tuesday and Thursday at 6 pm, Saturday at 9 am
  • Intro offer One week unlimited for $25
  • Drop-in $22
  • Monthly unlimited $129, or $99 with a student ID

Riverbend Heat Studio

Coralville, with a free parking lot. Calm and quiet, with a gentler style of heat from infrared panels.

  • Room temperature 85 to 95°F
  • Class styles Warm slow flow, yin, restorative
  • Beginner class Warm Flow Intro, Monday and Wednesday at 7 pm, Sunday at 11 am
  • Intro offer First class free
  • Drop-in $18
  • Monthly unlimited $109

Prairie Flame Yoga

North Liberty, about 15 minutes from downtown. The most athletic of the three, with early morning classes.

  • Room temperature Around 100°F
  • Class styles Power hot flow, yoga sculpt, hot pilates
  • Beginner class Flame Foundations, Saturday at 8 am
  • Intro offer Two weeks unlimited for $30
  • Drop-in $20
  • Monthly unlimited $119

Which studio is best for you

If you live on or near campus, Ember Hot Yoga is the obvious pick. You can walk there, it offers beginner classes three times a week, and the student rate makes a monthly membership more manageable.

If you are nervous about the heat, start at Riverbend Heat Studio. Its rooms run cooler, the classes move slowly, and your first class costs nothing, so there is very little to lose by trying it.

If you already work out and want a challenge, Prairie Flame Yoga is built for you. Its power and sculpt classes are fast and demanding, and the two-week intro gives you enough time to decide whether that pace suits you.

How we compared them

We started with the questions beginners ask before booking a first class. How hot does the room get? Is there a class meant for people who have never done this? What does it cost to try, and what does it cost to keep going? Location matters too, since a studio you have to drive to is harder to stick with once the weather turns cold.

Not ready to commit to any of them? Our guide to free hot yoga in Iowa City shows how to try all three for very little money, and our beginner's guide explains what to expect from your first class.
