Hot Yoga Gear for Beginners

You do not need much to start hot yoga, and you can rent almost everything at the studio for your first class. Once you know you are coming back, though, a few good pieces make the heat a lot easier to handle. Here is what is worth buying and what you can skip.

The best yoga mats for hot yoga for beginners

The biggest problem in a heated room is slipping. A mat that grips well in a cool class can turn slick once you start sweating, so look for a mat with a natural rubber base or a polyurethane top layer, which tend to stay grippy when wet. Avoid thin, smooth PVC mats, which are cheap but slide around in the heat.

Thickness matters less than grip. A mat around 4 to 5 millimeters thick cushions your knees without making balance poses wobbly. Expect to spend somewhere between $40 and $100 for a mat that holds up to regular hot classes. If that feels like a lot before you have committed, rent a mat at the studio for a few weeks first. All three studios in our hot yoga studios in Iowa City comparison rent mats for about $3.

Why a towel matters as much as the mat

A full-length yoga towel that lies on top of your mat soaks up sweat and gives your hands and feet something to grip. Many regulars say a good towel helps more than an expensive mat. Look for a microfiber towel sized to cover a full mat, ideally one with small silicone dots on the bottom so it does not bunch up. Lightly misting the towel with water before class helps it grip from the first pose. Bring a second small hand towel for your face.

What to wear to hot yoga

  • Close-fitting clothes. Loose shirts slide down in forward folds and fill with sweat. Fitted leggings or shorts and a fitted tank or sports top stay put.
  • Moisture-wicking fabric. Synthetic blends dry faster than cotton, which gets heavy once it is soaked.
  • Less than you think. Many people start in leggings and a T-shirt and switch to shorts within a few classes once they feel how warm the room gets.
  • No shoes or socks. Hot yoga is done barefoot, so all you need for your feet is the towel underneath them.

Water and the small extras

An insulated water bottle keeps water cold through a 60-minute class, which makes a real difference at 95 degrees. Bring more water than you think you need, and some people add an electrolyte drink after class. A hair tie, a bag for your damp clothes, and a change of clothes for the drive home round out the list.

What you can skip

Blocks and straps are provided at every studio we cover, so there is no need to buy your own. Yoga sculpt weights are provided too, as our what is yoga sculpt page explains. And skip anything marketed as a special hot yoga outfit until you know what you like to wear.

Start cheap, then upgrade
